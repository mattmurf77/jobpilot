# Resume Balance for a Stretch Application: IC Proof vs. Management Signal

*Provenance: web research (WebSearch/WebFetch) conducted 2026-08-04; sources dated 2020–2026, prioritizing 2023–2026. Compiled for a Lead PM (10 yrs, one fintech lending product, 1 direct report + informal mentoring, runs cadences) applying to "Head of Product" (req: 5+ yrs managing PM teams). Companion to `cover_letter_research_ic_to_manager_stretch.md`.*

## 1. What ratio do authorities actually recommend?

No source gives a hard percentage split — this is the first finding. Career-advice sites (Resumly.ai, RiResume, Enhancv, LinkedIn contributor posts, 2024–2026) converge instead on a **reframing mandate, not a subtraction mandate**: "your resume needs to tell a leadership story, not a technical contribution story" (Resumly.ai, "How to Reposition from Individual Contributor to Manager," 2024–2025). One useful non-resume proxy Resumly.ai cites: managers' actual time splits roughly 40–50% people, 30–40% strategy, 10–20% execution, vs. an IC's 80–90% execution — but the article does not translate this into bullet-count guidance, and no other source attempts to either. The Muse and Ask a Manager (askamanager.org, "a pain-free guide to writing a resume," 2023; "a bunch of questions and answers about your resume," Mar 2025) both frame the resume as "a marketing document... not a comprehensive account," with content chosen per-role — i.e., emphasis should be driven by the job posting's specific requirements, not a fixed formula. **Verdict on the sub-question: the "ratio" framing itself is not how the literature thinks about this** — it's about which content proves the specific gap (here: managing PM teams), not a general IC-vs-leadership dial.

## 2. What IC content stays vs. gets cut

Convergent guidance: **keep outcome/scale evidence, cut execution mechanics.** "Product launches with clear before-and-after metrics carry far more weight than descriptions of ongoing feature management" and resumes should "show product thinking, not just task execution" — describing the problem, trade-offs navigated, and business outcome, not the shipping steps (synthesis of Exponent/BeamJobs/scale.jobs PM-resume guides, 2025–2026). Executive-resume specialists frame this as a level shift: at IC level, verbs like "developed" or "supported" read as achievement; at leadership level, the same work should be re-verbed toward "led," "orchestrated," "drove" scale/outcome, not effort (Chameleon Resumes / thebiggamehunter.us, 2024–2025). Applied to this resume: the **scale bullet** (loan volume/portfolio numbers) and **0-to-1 launch bullet** (a launch with a business outcome) are exactly the "before/after metric" category that should stay, likely compressed to outcome-only. The **E2E lifecycle bullet** is closer to execution-mechanics — it's the best candidate for cutting or folding into one clause, since "resumes that only list launches" without a decision/tradeoff frame read as dated for a leadership audience.

## 3. Reframing without title inflation

The technique that recurs across sources is **result-first, scope-visible language, not new nouns**: "quantify achievements... revenue growth, cost reductions, ROI... be specific with numbers and timeframes" rather than inventing managerial vocabulary (Chameleon Resumes / Careers by Design, 2024–2026). For readiness signals specifically without a manager title, the caution is to draw on **documented de facto scope** — informal reports, review authority, workload/decision ownership — rather than implying formal headcount authority you didn't have (thebiggamehunter.us / Indeed career advice, 2024–2025, echoing the in-house-lawyer "no direct reports" guidance: "avoid emphasizing what you lack," frame what you actually did — reviewed others' work, trained, managed workload). This maps directly onto this resume's **team-repo/AI-rollout bullet** (built something others use — an "enabling systems" signal) and **operating-cadence bullet** (a genuine management behavior — running cadences — that should be labeled as such, not softened into a project bullet).

## 4. Counter-evidence: does IC delivery proof matter more?

This is real and shouldn't be waved away. Recruitment-economics literature: "past-performance appraisals are... much more valid in predicting future job performance" (internal-promotion research synthesis, business hiring literature, 2023–2025), and external-candidate credibility gaps are explicitly bridged with reference checks and work-sample proof, precisely because a resume alone under-proves execution ability (internal-vs-external hiring synthesis, 2024–2025). Separately, overselling readiness is flagged as a distinct failure mode: "experienced hiring managers are particularly good at detecting exaggerations... it's pretty easy to spot candidates who are over-inflating" (interview/hiring-manager-skepticism synthesis, 2024–2025), and HBR's *Women at Work* coaches warn that visibly "proving yourself... signals insecurity" (HBR podcast, Apr 2026, cited in the companion cover-letter research). **Both failure modes are real and symmetric**: a resume that's all thin management language with no delivery proof reads as unproven; a resume that's all execution detail with no leadership reframing reads as "not ready." Neither source base supports stripping delivery evidence to make room for management claims — the risk runs the other way if management content isn't backed by something concrete (cadences run, people mentored/promoted, decisions owned).

## 5. Synthesis and verdict for this resume

Recommended structure (1 page): **keep the scale bullet compressed to outcome numbers only; compress the E2E-lifecycle bullet into a subordinate clause or cut it (most execution-mechanical, least leadership-signaling); keep the 0-to-1 launch bullet but re-verb toward the business decision, not the shipping steps; keep and strengthen the team-repo/AI-rollout bullet as an "built a system others use" leadership-adjacent proof; keep and foreground the operating-cadence bullet explicitly as management behavior (who you run it for, what changed).** Add one bullet with quantified mentoring/informal-report outcomes if not already present — this is the single most-cited missing ingredient across sources.

**Verdict on the hypothesis ("fewer IC contributions, more manager content"):** Partially supported, precisely stated. The evidence supports **cutting execution *mechanics*** (the how-I-shipped-it detail in the E2E bullet) and **reframing, not deleting, the delivery-scale bullets** into leadership-legible language. It does **not** support wholesale de-emphasis of IC proof — delivery credibility remains the underlying trust signal, and thin, unearned management language is the more commonly cited failure mode in the counter-evidence. The correct move is compression + reframing of 1–2 bullets, not a proportional shift of the whole page.
