# AI-Writing Tells: A Sourced Catalog (Cover Letters, Resumes, Emails)

*Provenance: web research compiled 2026-08-04 from Wikipedia's WikiProject AI Cleanup "Signs of AI writing" page (fetched 2026-08-04), 2024-2026 corpus studies, editor/journalist commentary, and recruiter surveys.*

## 1. Lexical tells
**Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing"** (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing, catalogued from flagged drafts since 2023) tiers vocabulary by model era: **GPT-4 ('23-mid'24):** delve, boasts, bolstered, crucial, garner, intricate(cies), interplay, landscape, meticulous(ly), pivotal, underscore, tapestry, testament, vibrant. **GPT-4o (mid'24-mid'25):** align with, fostering, highlighting, showcasing, enhance. **GPT-5+ (mid'25 on):** emphasizing, enhance, highlighting, showcasing — narrower, harder to detect. Also flagged: significance-inflation phrases ("stands as a testament," "underscores the importance," "evolving landscape") and vague attribution ("industry reports," "experts argue," no named source).

**Corpus evidence:** A 2024 Science Advances/arXiv study of 15M+ PubMed abstracts (Kobak et al., arXiv:2406.07016) found 379 style words spiking abruptly post-ChatGPT, led by delve, showcasing, underscore, intricate, meticulous, boasts. A Stanford-linked analysis names **realm, intricate, showcasing, pivotal** as the four most diagnostic (via TopResume/Medium roundup, Jan 2026). A 2025 follow-up (arXiv:2502.09606) found "delve" usage dropped once mocked publicly in early 2024 — tells decay once named.

**Hiring-specific filler:** adept, tech-savvy, cutting-edge, and stock self-description ("results-driven, passionate, detail-oriented team player") flagged as content-free (AiApply Blog 2026; TopResume survey, May 2025, finding hiring managers spot AI resumes within ~20 seconds).

## 2. Structural tells
- **"Not just X, it's Y" / "It's not X — it's Y":** Wikipedia's top "negative parallelism" marker; a 2025 Substack field guide calls it the single most-cited AI structural tell — humans use it occasionally, LLMs "one per paragraph."
- **Tricolon addiction:** Wikipedia flags overuse of three-item lists; commentary (robotsatemyhomework.substack.com, 2025; Duey AI, 2025) attributes it to three being the statistical mode in training data — "one tricolon is elegant; three in a row is a pattern-recognition failure."
- **Copula avoidance:** swapping plain "is/are" for "serves as," "stands as," "functions as," "boasts" (Wikipedia).
- **Colon-headed bolded list openers** replacing prose (bullet + **bold header:** + description) — a Wikipedia formatting tell that maps onto cover letters with bolded "Leadership:" style headers.
- **"Despite challenges..." hedge-closer** ending on vague optimism — Wikipedia's challenges/future-prospects template, directly analogous to cover-letter closers like "While the market is competitive, I am confident...".
- **Em-dash density:** UCL's Andrew Gray (2024) found em-dash use climbing sharply in ChatGPT-era academic writing; industry estimates (Technically/Bieber 2025; Duey AI 2025) put AI output at 10-20+ em dashes/1,000 words vs. 2-3 for humans — both caution it's weak alone, easily gamed.
- **Elegant variation:** excessive synonym-swapping to avoid repeating a word (Wikipedia).

## 3. Rhythmic/tonal tells
- **Low "burstiness":** GPTZero's framework (through 2025) defines burstiness as sentence-length variance — humans score 0.6-1.2, GPT clusters 0.2-0.4; perplexity runs human ~80-100 vs. GPT-4 ~20-30, since uniform sentences are easier for a model to produce. Caveat: GPTZero itself moved off pure perplexity/burstiness scoring in autumn 2023, and newer models blur the signal.
- **Relentless polish, zero rough edges, no idiosyncratic detail:** the "texture" tell (Field Guide to AI Tells 2025; Cherryleaf 2026).
- **Formal-register creep / sycophantic genericness:** all-purpose self-description that "could fit any job" (AiApply 2026).

## 4. What matters most in hiring
- **Speed:** TopResume (May 2025) — hiring managers flag AI cover letters/resumes within ~20 seconds.
- **Genericness beats vocabulary as the #1 trigger:** recruiter commentary (Coursera roundup 2026; AiApply 2026) converges on template-matching and zero role/company specificity — "shouldn't echo what's already in the posting" — as the top real signal, more than any single word.
- **Business impact:** Robert Half survey (March 2026) — 67% of US HR leaders say screening AI applications has slowed hiring; 65% say it's harder to verify skills; **~1 in 5 recruiters would reject an obviously AI-generated application.**
- **Named hiring-specific words:** delve, realm, intricate, showcasing, pivotal, adept, tech-savvy, cutting-edge (Stanford-linked study via 2026 roundup).

## 5. Kill list — top 25 tells ranked by signal strength
1. **Generic "could-be-any-job" phrasing** ("results-driven, detail-oriented team player") — top recruiter trigger (AiApply 2026).
2. **"Not just X, it's Y"** — most-cited structural tell (Wikipedia; Field Guide 2025).
3. **Letter echoing the job posting back** — reads as copy-paste (Coursera 2026).
4. **delve** — most iconic AI word (Kobak et al. 2024; Wikipedia).
5. **showcasing/showcase** — persists across all three model eras (Wikipedia).
6. **pivotal** — Stanford top-4 word (TopResume 2026).
7. **realm** — Stanford top-4 word.
8. **intricate/intricacies** — Stanford top-4; Kobak 2024.
9. **tapestry** — "no innocent use" per editors (Wikipedia).
10. **testament to / stands as a testament** — significance-inflation tell (Wikipedia).
11. **underscores the importance of** — passive emphasis, no actor (Cherryleaf 2026).
12. **boasts (a)** — promotional-language tell (Wikipedia).
13. **meticulous(ly)** — GPT-4-era top word (Kobak et al. 2024).
14. **crucial / plays a crucial role** — cross-era survivor (Wikipedia).
15. **evolving landscape** — significance-inflation filler (Wikipedia).
16. **fostering/foster** — GPT-4o-era top word (Wikipedia).
17. **align(s) with** — GPT-4o-era tell (Wikipedia).
18. **enhance** — rare word surviving into GPT-5 era (Wikipedia).
19. **"in today's fast-paced world"** — temporal cliché opener (2026 editor blogs).
20. **Tricolon addiction** (three-item lists everywhere) — statistical-mode default (robotsatemyhomework.substack.com 2025).
21. **Copula avoidance** ("serves as" for "is") — structural tell (Wikipedia).
22. **Bolded colon-list openers** in narrative prose — formatting tell (Wikipedia).
23. **"Despite challenges..." hedge-closer** — maps to cover-letter closers (Wikipedia).
24. **Uniform sentence length (low burstiness)** — GPTZero framework 2023-2025.
25. **Em-dash overuse** (10-20+/1,000 words) — Gray/UCL 2024; weak alone, strong clustered.

**Cross-cutting caveat:** every source agrees no single tell is reliable alone — signal lives in *clustering/density*, and publicized tells (e.g. "delve") decay in frequency once named, as models and writers adapt (arXiv:2502.09606, 2025).
