# AI Detectors That Explain Their Verdict — Free-Tier Usability Research

**Provenance:** Hands-on visits to live product pages/UIs on 2026-08-05 (gptzero.me, sapling.ai, zerogpt.com, quillbot.com, copyleaks.com — read via browser, current on-page copy/pricing quoted directly; live scan execution succeeded on ZeroGPT/Sapling's own sample, was blocked by UI automation issues on GPTZero this session) + WebSearch of 2025-2026 reviews, academic papers, and vendor pages for tools not directly browsed (Scribbr, Winston AI, Originality.ai, Grammarly, Copyleaks pricing detail, Phrasly/Undetectable, GLTR). Secondhand claims are marked as such.

## Per-tool findings

**GPTZero** (hands-on, gptzero.me) — Free: 10,000 characters, no account for a single scan (account needed only past that or for uploads). Output: overall %, plus **sentence-level color-coded highlights** and, per their FAQ, natural-language explanations of *why* a passage was flagged — GPTZero markets this explicitly as its differentiator ("the only detector that explains exactly why text is AI with natural language"). API exists (developer tier, paid). Accuracy: GPTZero claims 99%/95.7% on the RAID benchmark; independently, Pangram Labs' 2026 test of 30 detectors against ChatGPT-4o/Gemini 2.0/Claude 3.7 samples scored GPTZero 7/9 AI-sample detection, 3/3 on human text — solid but not perfect. Privacy: GPTZero states a "Zero-Log" no-training policy on one policy page, but secondhand sources (2026) report conflicting language elsewhere suggesting text may be retained/used for model improvement outside enterprise plans — treat as unresolved/contradictory, verify before pasting sensitive material.

**Sapling AI Detector** (hands-on, sapling.ai) — No sign-up needed for basic checks; free tier is capped at shorter queries ("AI detector — longer queries" is gated to Pro, $25/mo). Output goes beyond a single score: it shows per-sentence highlighting plus a **"Show Sentence Perplexity Scores"** toggle exposing the actual token-level statistic driving the verdict — the most technically transparent free explanation of any tool checked. API: metered, paid, documented at sapling.ai/pricing.

**ZeroGPT** (hands-on, zerogpt.com) — Free: 15,000 characters, no account required ("0/15,000 Characters... Check 350,000 characters, Upgrade Here" for more). Explicitly advertises "Highlighted Sentences — every sentence written by AI is highlighted, with a gauge." Has a public API (paid). Reputation: widely cited in 2025-2026 comparison blogs as having a comparatively high false-positive rate on human text versus GPTZero/Pangram (secondhand, multiple 2026 reviews).

**QuillBot AI Detector** (hands-on, quillbot.com; same detection engine reportedly powers Scribbr's checker) — Free: up to 1,200 words/scan, 6 scans/day; account required to use it at all ("Create account" gate on the free tier). Explicitly provides **"explainer cards" with insights into why text was flagged**, though the free tier gives only "limited explainer cards" vs. unlimited on Premium ($). Claims 99% accuracy per RAID (their own citation, secondhand-style marketing claim).

**Scribbr AI Detector** (secondhand, 2026 reviews) — Free: 1,200 words/scan, unlimited scans/day, no account. Free tier only detects older models (GPT-2/3/3.5); GPT-4o/Claude/Gemini-era detection requires a paid Scribbr plan. Gives a percentage plus highlighting, powered by the QuillBot-style perplexity/burstiness engine.

**Copyleaks** (hands-on for marketing copy; limits secondhand) — "Start for Free" requires an account; reported free allowance ~10 pages/~2,500 words/month, with some sources citing up to 25,000 characters without an account for spot checks (conflicting secondhand figures — verify at signup). Its **"AI Logic"** feature is a genuine explain-why system: "AI Phrases" flag specific spans with the statistical AI-likelihood behind them, and "AI Source Match" flags text matching known AI outputs elsewhere. Full API available (paid, enterprise-oriented). Claims 99% accuracy "backed by independent third-party studies."

**Winston AI** (secondhand, 2026 reviews) — 14-day free trial (~2,000-word credits, no card), plus a ~2,000-character no-signup basic scan. Reviews describe a "sentence-level evidence map," but it's unclear whether that's available in the free/trial tier or gated to paid.

**Originality.ai** (secondhand) — No real ongoing free tier; a short-snippet demo scan with no signup exists, but sustained use requires paid credits (1 credit ≈ 100 words; Base plan $14.95/mo). Has a documented API. Pangram Labs' 2026 test scored it 7/9 on AI samples.

**Grammarly** (secondhand) — Its "Authorship" feature is **not a text classifier**; it's a real-time keystroke/paste logger inside Grammarly's own editor, so it can't evaluate text written elsewhere (a pasted cover letter draft gets nothing). Grammarly's separate standalone AI checker gives only a percentage, no highlighting. Notably, Pangram Labs' 2026 30-detector test found Grammarly's detector caught **0 of 9** AI-generated samples.

**Phrasly / Undetectable AI style tools** (secondhand) — These are humanizer-first products; free tiers (e.g., Phrasly: 550 humanization words, "basic AI detection bypass") exist to funnel into paid "bypass detection" plans, not to explain why text reads as AI. Not fit for this use case, and arguably counterproductive for a job seeker (using a "bypass" tool to disguise AI-flavored writing is a worse fix than actually rewriting the sentence).

**GLTR / open-source** (secondhand) — GLTR (MIT-IBM/HarvardNLP) is the original visual per-token perplexity highlighter and is on Hugging Face (MIT license), fully free/local/no account. But it's built around GPT-2-era statistics and is described in 2026 sources as unreliable for modern GPT-4o/Claude/Gemini-era text without adaptation — better as a teaching tool than a practical checker today.

## Synthesis

**(a) Top free picks for "explain why," not just a score:**
1. **Sapling** — free, no account, per-sentence perplexity scores visible on demand — the most literal "show your work" of the free tools.
2. **GPTZero** — free 10k-character scan, no account, sentence highlighting + named-pattern explanations; best-known brand, decent independent scores, but check the privacy-policy inconsistency first.
3. **QuillBot** (or Scribbr, same engine) — free explainer cards on flagged spans; QuillBot's 1,200-word/6-scans-a-day cap fits a repeated-editing workflow for a ~400-word letter, though it requires an account.

**(b) Caveats — read before trusting any result:** Detectors disagree with each other constantly — the same paragraph can score 0% on one tool and 70%+ on another (evidenced above by QuillBot vs. ZeroGPT anecdotes and by Pangram Labs' 2026 head-to-head, where Grammarly missed all 9 AI samples that GPTZero and Originality.ai mostly caught). None of this is proof of authorship in either direction: OpenAI shut down its own AI-text classifier in 2023 for unacceptably low accuracy, and Liang et al.'s widely-cited study found detectors falsely flagged 61.3% of non-native-English TOEFL essays as AI-written versus near-zero for native-speaker essays — a bias later research says is "sufficiently mitigated" only when non-native writing is well-represented in training data, which not every vendor discloses. Treat any single score — 0% or 90% — as a signal to reread a sentence, never as a verdict to act on alone, and never as grounds to accuse someone else.

**(c) Free API for automation:** None of the tools with genuinely free tiers (Sapling free, GPTZero free, ZeroGPT free, QuillBot free, Scribbr free) offer a *free* API — all API access (GPTZero, Sapling, ZeroGPT, Copyleaks, Originality.ai) is metered/paid. For a local automation loop that repeatedly checks cover-letter drafts, the realistic free path is scripting against the web UI (fragile, ToS-questionable) or accepting a small per-call cost on a metered API like ZeroGPT's or Originality.ai's rather than expecting a no-cost automation tier.
