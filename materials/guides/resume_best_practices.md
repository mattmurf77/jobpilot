# Resume Best Practices — Senior+ Product Management

A working reference for writing and iterating a Senior PM / Group PM / Director-of-Product resume targeting Big Tech, enterprise B2B SaaS, sportsbooks/iGaming, AI, and sports-management technology. Built from 2026 hiring research, ATS/AI-screening data, and PM-specific resume guides.

> This file is the consolidated best-practices reference JobPilot's tailoring engine and review checks were built around. The scoring rubric graders use is `resume_scorecard.md`; deeper research notes live in the repo's `research/` folder. For translating company-internal language before sending externally, see **`resume_generalization_guide.md`**.

---

## 1. Core Principles (read these first)

1. **Outcomes over duties.** Every bullet should answer "what changed because of you?" — recruiters scan for impact, not job descriptions.
2. **Quantify everything you can.** Numbers, percentages, dollars, scale. Bullets without metrics raise the question "did this actually move anything?"
3. **Action-Verb + Task + Result formula.** Strong verb → what you did → measurable result. Example: *"Spearheaded redesign of pricing flow that lifted enterprise conversion 31% and added $4.2M ARR in 6 months."*
4. **Active voice, never passive.** "Launched" not "was responsible for launching." Avoid "Helped with," "Worked on," "Tasked with," "Duties included," "Responsible for."
5. **Tailor every submission.** The single biggest mistake recruiters cite in 2026 is generic resumes; 48% will auto-dismiss a non-tailored resume/cover letter.
6. **Write for two readers — the ATS/AI screener AND the human.** They want different things; you must satisfy both.
7. **One page is the user's stated preference.** Senior+ research generally accepts 2 pages, but a tight 1-page resume forces ruthless prioritization and reads as confident at the Director level. *Trade-off note: if you have 10+ years and multiple distinct product areas, 2 pages is defensible — but bullets must stay surgical.*

## 2. Page, Length & Layout

### Length
- **Target: 1 page** (per your stated preference). Forces you to cut weakest bullets and amplify strategic ones.
- **2-page exception:** acceptable for 10+ years with multi-domain scope; ensure page 1 stands alone (recruiter often reads only page 1).
- Never go to 3 pages unless it's a CV for academic/research roles.

### Format
- **Single-column layout.** ATS parsers fail on multi-column, tables, text boxes, headers/footers, sidebars, icons, charts, skill bars, and graphics. These cause data to be misread or dropped entirely.
- **Native PDF or DOCX** with selectable text (no scanned/flattened PDFs).
- **Standard fonts only:** Arial, Calibri, Helvetica, Times New Roman, Georgia, Garamond. 10–12 pt body, 14–18 pt name.
- **Margins:** 0.5"–1" on all sides. Don't go below 0.5".
- **Section headers** must be standard and recognizable: `Experience`, `Education`, `Skills`, `Summary`, `Projects`. ATS parses on these labels.
- **Dates:** consistent format (MMM YYYY – MMM YYYY or MM/YYYY). Inconsistency confuses the parser.
- **Bullets:** standard solid round bullets only. No emoji, no Wingdings symbols.
- **Test your format:** copy/paste into plain text — if structure collapses, ATS will too.

## 3. Section-by-Section Anatomy

### Header
- Full name (largest text on page)
- City, State (no full street address — privacy + ATS doesn't need it)
- Phone, professional email
- LinkedIn URL (custom slug), portfolio/personal site if relevant
- *Don't include:* photo, DOB, marital status, full address, secondary email

### Professional Summary (3–4 lines, optional but recommended at Senior+)
- Title + years of experience + 1–2 most distinctive credentials.
- Anchor with a flagship metric or scope claim.
- Match the exact target title where possible (if the JD says "Senior Product Manager," echo it).
- **Example:** *"Group Product Manager with 9+ years shipping AI-powered consumer and B2B SaaS products. Led $40M ARR portfolio across 4 product lines, built/mentored 3 PMs, and delivered 0→1 LLM features used by 8M+ MAU. Track record across regulated environments (sportsbook, fintech) and platform/marketplace dynamics."*
- 2–4 sentences max. Don't restate the rest of the resume — frame it.

### Experience (the meat — 60–70% of the page)
- Reverse-chronological.
- Header line: **Company** — Location | **Title** | Dates.
- 1–2 line context sentence under each role optional (helps recruiter who doesn't know the company): "Series C HR-tech SaaS, ~250 FTE, $40M ARR."
- 3–6 bullets per role; most recent role gets the most. Older roles get fewer (1–3).
- **Bullet anatomy:** Action verb → scope/what → outcome with metric → (optional) mechanism/strategic context.
  - *Strong:* "Drove 0→1 launch of AI-assist sports-betting feature across iOS/Android, lifting handle per active user 18% and reducing customer support volume 22% in first 90 days; owned roadmap, pricing, and GTM with 14-eng squad."
  - *Weak:* "Worked on AI feature for sports betting app."
- Lead with the **biggest, most recent, most relevant** wins. Recruiters spend 6–8 seconds on first scan.

### Skills / Tools
- Group by category: *Product Strategy & Discovery, Analytics & Experimentation, Tools, Technical, Domain.*
- List specific tool names by brand (Jira, Linear, Productboard, Amplitude, Mixpanel, Looker, SQL, Figma, GitHub, Notion). ATS matches on exact strings.
- Include **both acronym and full form** for searched terms ("Product-Led Growth (PLG)," "Generative AI / GenAI / LLM").
- Don't include outdated/irrelevant filler (e.g., "Microsoft Office" reads as a red flag in 2026).

### Education
- Degree, school, year. GPA only if 3.7+ and within ~5 years of graduation.
- Relevant certs: PMC, Reforge, Pragmatic, Stanford GSB, AWS, etc.
- Skip high school.

### Optional sections (use only if they strengthen the case)
- **Selected Projects / Side Bets:** for AI PMs, OSS, indie launches, hackathons.
- **Patents / Publications:** for technical/AI roles.
- **Awards / Speaking:** for senior visibility.
- **Languages:** only if business-relevant.

## 4. Quantifying Impact — and What to Do When You Can't

### Metric categories for PMs (use as a checklist)
- **Revenue / business:** ARR, MRR, deal size, win rate, attach rate, GMV, take rate, handle (sportsbook), LTV.
- **Growth:** MAU/DAU/WAU, signups, activation rate, viral coefficient, organic share.
- **Retention / engagement:** D1/D7/D30, churn, NRR, expansion, session length, sessions per user.
- **Conversion:** funnel step lift, CTR, CVR, add-to-cart, checkout completion.
- **Quality / efficiency:** P95 latency, defect rate, ticket deflection, NPS/CSAT, time-to-resolution.
- **Cost / ops:** cost-per-X, infra savings, COGS reduction, support cost reduction.
- **Team / scope:** team size led, # of PMs/engineers, # of product lines, geographies, stakeholders, customers, # of releases/yr.

### When the metric isn't yours or is confidential
Use these credible substitutes — all defensible in interviews:
- **Scope proxies:** "Owned roadmap for 4 product lines representing ~$40M ARR." "Led discovery across 6 enterprise design partners."
- **Velocity / cadence:** "Shipped 11 major releases in 12 months across 3 surfaces." "Cut release cycle from quarterly to bi-weekly."
- **Reach:** "Used by 8M+ MAU across 14 markets." "Adopted by 47 of top-50 enterprise customers."
- **Decision rights:** "Sole product owner of pricing and packaging." "Final approver on $X budget allocation."
- **Forcing functions:** "Reduced exec-escalation tickets to zero over 2 quarters."
- **Customer voice:** "Lifted G2 rating from 4.1 to 4.6 over 3 quarters."

### Hard-to-quantify impact — informal leadership, coverage, skip-level work
This is the senior-PM resume's hardest writing problem. Tactics that work:

1. **Name the scope explicitly** — team size, budget, # of skip-level reports influenced, # of cross-functional partners.
2. **Use the situation→action→result frame inside one bullet.**
   - *"Stepped in for Director of Product during 4-month parental leave; ran weekly business review for 3-PM team, owned $12M planning cycle, and shipped 2 OKR-defining launches with no slip."*
3. **Promote yourself in the bullet language.** If you ran the meeting, say "led." If you set the agenda, say "drove." If you owned the outcome, say "owned."
4. **Translate informal influence into outcomes.**
   - *"Without direct authority, aligned 3 engineering orgs and 2 design teams on shared platform RFC, unblocking 4 dependent roadmaps and shaving an estimated 6 months off cross-org delivery."*
5. **Use comparative scope.** "Operated at scope typical of 1 level up — ran QBRs, owned hiring loop, managed cross-functional stakeholder map of 30+."
6. **Cite recognition or formal markers.** Promotion timing ("promoted twice in 3 years"), nominations, awards, retention of direct/indirect reports, calibration outcomes.

### Showing career progression at one company
Three formats — pick by fit:

- **Stacked entries** — best when role evolved but core remit is similar:
  ```
  Acme — San Francisco, CA
    Senior Product Manager (Mar 2024 – Present)
    Product Manager (Jul 2021 – Mar 2024)
    Associate Product Manager (Jun 2019 – Jul 2021)
      • Bullet covering the most senior + recent work first
      • Bullets that span the arc, with promotion noted explicitly
  ```
- **Separate entries** — best when scope/remit changed materially. Each title gets its own block of bullets.
- **Single entry, promotion noted in a bullet** — only when title changed but day-to-day didn't.

In all cases, **call out the promotion explicitly** in a bullet: *"Promoted to Senior PM within 18 months for shipping flagship feature 6 weeks ahead of plan."* And reference the arc in the **Summary**: *"Promoted twice in 4 years across IC and lead-PM tracks."*

## 5. Action Verbs — the working palette

Lead with these (PM-relevant, high-impact, 2026 recruiter-tested):

**Strategy / vision:** Spearheaded, Pioneered, Defined, Reframed, Established, Shaped, Architected, Conceived, Set
**Execution / launch:** Launched, Shipped, Delivered, Drove, Executed, Operationalized, Productized, Rolled out, Scaled
**Growth / impact:** Accelerated, Grew, Tripled, Doubled, Lifted, Increased, Expanded, Unlocked, Captured
**Optimization:** Reduced, Cut, Streamlined, Eliminated, Compressed, Simplified, Consolidated, Automated
**Leadership:** Led, Built, Mentored, Coached, Hired, Managed, Directed, Championed, Influenced, Aligned
**Discovery / insight:** Uncovered, Diagnosed, Validated, Identified, Surfaced, Tested, Synthesized, Ran (research)
**Cross-functional:** Partnered, Collaborated, Coordinated, Negotiated, Brokered, Unblocked

**Avoid these tired or weak verbs:** Helped, Worked on, Assisted, Participated in, Was involved in, Handled, Tasked with, Responsible for, Oversaw (often weak — prefer "led" or "owned"), Utilized (use "used"), Liaised.

## 6. ATS & AI Resume Screening — How to Get Past the Bots

### How parsing works (2026 stack)
- ATS converts the file to raw structured data, stripping formatting.
- It extracts: name, contact, titles, employers, dates, skills, education, certifications.
- Pattern recognition relies on standard section headers, date formats, bullet structures.
- Modern ATS layers on **semantic matching** (synonyms — "revenue growth" ≈ "sales performance"), **job title matching**, and **skills taxonomy by seniority level**.
- AI screeners increasingly score **achievement density** — what % of bullets contain numbers/results.

### Stat reality (2026)
- ~75% of resumes filtered before a human reads them.
- 83% of companies use some form of AI in screening.
- 87% ATS compatibility on AI-assisted resumes vs ~72% on manual; 78% keyword alignment vs 52%.

### Optimization rules
1. **Mirror exact JD wording.** If JD says "go-to-market," don't say "GTM" only — use both. ATS doesn't always equate synonyms.
2. **Echo the target title.** If JD says "Senior Product Manager," use that exact phrase in the summary.
3. **Spell out + acronym** for skills: "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)," "Generative AI (GenAI)," "Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)."
4. **Place keywords in multiple zones:** summary, skills, experience bullets. Never stuff — modern AI flags repetition.
5. **Use standard section headers.** Don't get cute ("Where I've Made Magic" → use "Experience").
6. **Avoid invisible white-text keyword stuffing.** Modern parsers and AI flag and discard for it; some reject the resume.
7. **Quantify densely.** Some AI screeners specifically score "achievement density" — bullets with hard numbers.
8. **Submit .docx or text-based PDF.** Avoid Pages exports, scanned PDFs, or designs from Canva/Figma without flattening to text-selectable PDF.
9. **Test before you send.** Tools: Jobscan, Resume Worded, SkillSyncer, Enhancv, Resume.io ATS checker.
10. **Mirror the JD's phrasing of competencies** but in your own bullets — semantic AI rewards alignment, not duplication.

### What AI graders look for in PM resumes specifically
- **Hard disqualifiers** (e.g., years of experience, work auth, must-have skill).
- **Must-haves** (2–3 capabilities the role can't survive without).
- **Strong signals** (relevant scale, tool stack, domain).
- **Achievement density** (how many bullets carry quantified outcomes).
- **Title/seniority ladder match.**
- **Tenure pattern** (avg time per role; very short stints flagged).
- **Recency of relevant experience.**

## 7. PM-Specific Resume Signals

### What hiring managers across PM roles consistently look for
- **Outcomes** — revenue, growth, retention, cost, NPS — over feature lists.
- **Scope clarity** — did you own a feature, a surface, a product, a portfolio?
- **Cross-functional leadership without authority** — engineering, design, data, sales, CS, legal, marketing.
- **Strategy & roadmap ownership** — vision setting, prioritization framework, trade-off decisions.
- **Customer obsession evidence** — qual + quant research, win/loss, JTBD.
- **Data fluency** — A/B testing, SQL, analytics tools, metrics design.
- **Shipping velocity** — cadence and consistency.
- **Judgment under ambiguity** — 0→1, pivots, sunsetting.

### Senior+ / Director signals (what differentiates from Sr. PM)
- Scaling impact, not just shipping features.
- People leadership: hired, leveled, calibrated, performance-managed.
- Org influence: shaped strategy, ran QBRs, drove planning cycles, owned narratives at exec level.
- Portfolio thinking: trade-offs across multiple products / streams.
- Hiring & talent density: who you've built and grown.
- Stakeholder altitude: comfort with C-suite, board, customers' execs.
- P&L or budget authority where applicable.

### Common PM keyword set (work into Skills + bullets where true)
Product strategy · Roadmapping · Product vision · OKRs · KPIs · Discovery · User research · Customer interviews · JTBD · A/B testing · Experimentation · SQL · Amplitude · Mixpanel · Looker · Tableau · Figma · Jira · Linear · Productboard · Confluence · Notion · Agile · Scrum · Kanban · RICE · MoSCoW · Kano · Go-to-market (GTM) · PLG · 0→1 · Platform · Marketplace · Pricing & packaging · Monetization · Activation · Retention · Funnel optimization · LLMs · Generative AI · Prompt engineering · RAG · Evaluation harnesses · API products · Stakeholder management · Cross-functional leadership.

## 8. Industry-Specific Slants (your target verticals)

### Big Tech / FAANG-tier
- Scale numbers matter: MAU, geos, languages, devices.
- Show **mechanism** — how you got the outcome (which decision, which experiment, which trade-off).
- Demonstrate cross-functional alignment with **sister orgs** at large company scale.
- Title alignment matters; "Senior Product Manager" vs "Lead PM" carries different leveling signals at Google/Meta/Amazon.
- Recruiters expect tight resumes, very heavy on quantified outcomes, and clean formatting.
- Highlight any past FAANG experience, principal-stakeholder names, well-known launches.

### Enterprise / B2B SaaS
- Hiring managers care about: ARR impact, churn reduction, expansion, NRR, deal size.
- Speak the language: SSO, SCIM, RBAC, audit logs, compliance (SOC 2, HIPAA, GDPR), tenancy, multi-region, enterprise readiness.
- Show GTM and CS partnership — pricing, packaging, contracts, design partner programs.
- Show stakeholder management at the **buyer altitude** (CIO, CISO, VP Ops).
- Frameworks: PLG vs sales-led, land-and-expand, value-based pricing.

### Sportsbooks / iGaming
- Domain credibility: trading, risk, liability, hold/handle, parlay, in-play, bonusing, KYC/AML, geofencing, responsible gambling, jurisdictional licensing (NJ, NV, MGA, UKGC).
- Regulated-environment chops: compliance partners, certified releases, state-by-state launches, audit trails.
- B2C high-velocity experimentation: large traffic, daily decisions, real-time pricing, event-driven UX.
- Outcomes that matter: handle, hold %, GGR/NGR, monthly actives, deposit conversion, retention, CAC payback, cross-sell to casino.
- Tooling: Kambi/SBTech-equivalent stacks, feature flags at scale, A/B at low p-values quickly.

### AI / LLM PM
- Production AI signal: "shipped LLM-powered feature in production used by N users."
- Prompt engineering, eval harness building, model selection trade-offs (cost/latency/quality), guardrails, hallucination mitigation, RAG, vector DBs.
- Capacity to talk to ML eng about precision/recall, eval sets, cost-per-token, P95 latency.
- A/B testing for non-deterministic outputs; offline/online eval design.
- Specific stack names: OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, Llama, LangChain, LlamaIndex, Pinecone, Weaviate, Vertex, Bedrock.
- Beware: don't claim AI experience you can't defend in interviews — graders and interviewers cross-check.

### Sports management technology
- Real-time data platforms, broadcast, fan engagement, ticketing, league/team operations.
- Multi-stakeholder products: leagues, broadcasters, sponsors, athletes, fans.
- Live-event constraints: latency, reliability under spike load, broadcast windows.
- Quantify reach: # of leagues, # of teams, # of fans, broadcast hours, viewer engagement.

## 9. Common Mistakes & Red Flags Recruiters Auto-Reject On

- Typos / grammar errors — 61% of hiring managers auto-disqualify on a single typo.
- Generic, untailored resume — 48% auto-dismiss.
- Listing duties instead of achievements.
- LinkedIn ↔ resume mismatch — 65% of hiring managers see resumes that don't match LinkedIn; treat this as a screening risk.
- Unexplained large gaps — fine to have; not fine to leave unaddressed.
- Outdated skills (Microsoft Office, "internet research") signal out-of-touch.
- Photos, age, marital status — illegal/unprofessional in US context.
- Multi-column / sidebars / icons — break ATS.
- Skill bars / percent meters — meaningless to humans, broken to ATS.
- Inflated/false claims — cross-checked at reference and even years post-hire.
- Cliché summary opening: "Results-driven professional with passion for…"
- Buzzword soup with no evidence ("synergistic," "rockstar," "ninja," "guru").

## 10. Pre-Submit Checklist

Run this every time before you submit. (Hard gate — no exceptions.)

**Content**
- [ ] Tailored to this specific JD (title, keywords, scope).
- [ ] Top-of-page summary echoes the target title.
- [ ] Every bullet starts with a strong action verb.
- [ ] Every bullet has a measurable outcome OR a defensible scope proxy.
- [ ] No "Responsible for / Worked on / Helped with."
- [ ] Most recent + most senior role gets the most bullets.
- [ ] Career progression (promotions) explicitly visible.

**ATS / AI**
- [ ] Single-column layout, no tables/text boxes/images.
- [ ] Standard section headers.
- [ ] Consistent date format.
- [ ] Native PDF or DOCX, text is selectable.
- [ ] Exact JD keywords appear in summary, skills, AND bullets (no stuffing).
- [ ] Acronyms paired with full forms.
- [ ] Run through Jobscan / Resume Worded / Enhancv ATS check.
- [ ] Copy/paste into plain text — structure still readable.

**Polish**
- [ ] Zero typos / grammar errors (read aloud + run Grammarly).
- [ ] LinkedIn matches resume (titles, dates, scope).
- [ ] Filename is professional: `FirstLast_Resume_Company.pdf`.
- [ ] Length matches target (1 page per stated preference; 2 page only if justified).
- [ ] All hyperlinks (LinkedIn, portfolio) work.
- [ ] Phone, email, location are current.

## 11. Senior+ Specific Reminders

- Lead with **scope and outcome**, not features. Director-level resumes that read like Sr. PM resumes get downleveled.
- Show **people leadership** explicitly: hired N, mentored N, leveled N, retained N.
- Use **portfolio language** when relevant: "owned 4 product lines representing $40M ARR."
- Demonstrate **org influence**: planning cycles, exec-level narratives, board/customer-exec exposure.
- Compress older roles aggressively — your last 5 years should dominate.
- Don't undersell. At Group PM / Director, claiming "led" when you led is not bragging, it's accuracy. The bar is "could you defend this bullet in a 30-minute behavioral?"
